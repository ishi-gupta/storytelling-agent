Genre: literary culinary thriller with magical realism
Place: Mexico City (Roma Norte restaurant; Xochimilco chinampas; San Rafael markets; government labs)
Time: present day
Theme: ambition vs. ethics; cultural stewardship; memory and food; exploitation and scarcity; science vs. tradition
Tone: sensory, tense, unsentimental, morally ambiguous
Point of View: close third on Ana Morales, occasional brief interludes from Itzel Xóchitl (first person) for contrast
Characters:  - Ana Morales, 34, chef-owner of Morada, talented, cash-strapped, disciplined - Itzel Xóchitl, 50s, chinampa farmer and guardian of a pre-Hispanic cultivation practice - Diego Ramos, 42, venture-backed investor pushing expansion - Dr. Valeria Chen, 39, food chemist at UNAM, cautious, pragmatic - Mateo Juárez, 28, sous-chef, loyal, observant - Carmen Morales, 62, Ana’s mother with early-onset Alzheimer’s - Inspector Luis Aranda, 45, health authority with a reputation for clean raids - Mikel Arriaga, 51, Basque food critic with a large platform
Premise:  - Morada is weeks from closing; suppliers demand cash; a no-star review looms. - Ana sources herbs in Xochimilco; Itzel serves her a broth infused with “flor de noche” pollen, a rare seasonal bloom cultivated on one chinampa with ritual constraints. The taste triggers sharp, specific memories; Ana’s mother briefly recognizes her after tasting a sample. - Ana builds a dish—charred quelites, amaranth aguachile, and a microdose of the pollen—that causes diners to recall vivid personal moments; word-of-mouth lines the block; Mikel’s review calls it “a memory engine.” - Diego pressures Ana to secure exclusive supply and scale; offers capital contingent on predictable output. Itzel refuses mass harvesting; warns that overuse drains the plant and unsettles “the agreements” of the chinampa.
