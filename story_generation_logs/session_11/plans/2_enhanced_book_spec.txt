Genre: literary culinary thriller with magical realism 
Place: Mexico City (Roma Norte restaurant on Colima near Plaza Río de Janeiro; Xochimilco chinampas in San Gregorio Atlapulco, night harvests from a protected plot off the Cuemanco canal; San Rafael markets around Mercado San Cosme and Calle Serapio Rendón stalls; government labs at UNAM Instituto de Química in Ciudad Universitaria and InDRE in Tlalpan; Cofepris offices in Nápoles; Ana’s cramped walk-up in Doctores; Carmen’s public clinic near Hospital General) 
Time: present day (rainy season through Day of the Dead; water shortages, rolling brownouts, occasional canal closures after contamination alerts) 
Theme: ambition vs. ethics; cultural stewardship; memory and food; exploitation and scarcity; science vs. tradition 
Tone: sensory, tense, unsentimental, morally ambiguous 
Point of View: close third on Ana Morales, occasional brief interludes from Itzel Xóchitl (first person) for contrast 
Characters:  - Ana Morales, 34, chef-owner of Morada, talented, cash-strapped, disciplined; cooks on a scorched plancha, keeps a ledger with grease-stained notes; sleeps in the office; burns on her forearms; debt with two suppliers and back rent. - Itzel Xóchitl, 50s, chinampa farmer and guardian of a pre-Hispanic cultivation practice; speaks softly, carries a machete wrapped in cloth; maintains one ritual plot with “flor de noche”; distrusts cameras; keeps a ledger of “agreements” with the canal and birds. - Diego Ramos, 42, venture-backed investor pushing expansion; runs a ghost-kitchen portfolio; always on the phone; proposes a lab-grown “essence” deal; hides a botany grad-school dropout past. - Dr. Valeria Chen, 39, food chemist at UNAM, cautious, pragmatic; runs a small olfaction lab; files everything; knows Cofepris processes; keeps her own mother’s blood pressure pills in her desk. - Mateo Juárez, 28, sous-chef, loyal, observant; ex-baker with steady hands; sleeps on a cot after double shifts; rides a beat-up Italika; records service notes on his phone. - Carmen Morales, 62, Ana’s mother with early-onset Alzheimer’s; ex-cafeteria cook; lucid flashes triggered by smells; wanders; hums boleros when anxious; fixates on a mole recipe she can’t fully recall. - Inspector Luis Aranda, 45, health authority with a reputation for clean raids; hates theatrics; carries a laminated checklist and a reputation for not taking bribes; diabetic, never eats on raids. - Mikel Arriaga, 51, Basque food critic with a large platform; sharp palate, sharper pen; cycles the city at dawn; publishes data-heavy pieces; has a personal history with memory loss in his family. 
Premise:  - Morada is weeks from closing; suppliers demand cash; an unstarred “promising but unfocused” review from Mikel is scheduled. A compressor dies mid-service; Ana pawns her knife roll to cover ice. - In Xochimilco, Itzel serves Ana a broth infused with “flor de noche” pollen, a rare bloom harvested by moonlight when egrets settle. The taste triggers granular recall—smells of Ana’s childhood stove, her father’s aftershave. A take-home vial leads to Carmen briefly recognizing Ana and reciting, near-verbatim, a lost salsa ratio. - Ana builds a dish—charred quelites, amaranth aguachile, and a microdose of the pollen—that causes diners to recall vivid, specific personal moments. Lines form; a thread on Mexican Twitter calls it “comer recuerdos.” Mikel’s revised piece dubs it “a memory engine.”
