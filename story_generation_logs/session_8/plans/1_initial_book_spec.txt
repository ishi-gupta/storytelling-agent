Genre: Contemporary Romance, Corporate Mystery 
Place: New York City (SoHo HQ, DUMBO coworking spaces, Midtown boardrooms, Brooklyn Heights, the High Line) 
Time: Present day (next 12–18 months) 
Theme: Trust vs. ambition; ethics in tech; surveillance and consent; whistleblowing; immigrant hustle; media responsibility 
Tone: Grounded, tense, smart, emotionally intimate 
Point of View: Dual first-person, alternating chapters (Mira Das and Daniel Park) 
Characters: Mira Das—senior product designer at QuillForge, ambitious, ethics-pricked; Daniel Park—investigative tech reporter, guarded, principled; Jonah Reed—contract UX researcher who flags “Grey Thread,” vanishes; Marco Alvarez—Head of Growth with shell LLC ties; Evelyn Cho—bank’s counsel who brokers their meeting; Lila Chen—QuillForge CEO, optics-first; Arman Petrov—CTO, protective of AutoDraft; Gabriel Ruiz—Sable Ridge front man linking fake “pilots”; Rosa Kim—Daniel’s editor, legal-savvy, deadline-brutal
Premise:  - On the night QuillForge launches AutoDraft AI at a SoHo rooftop event, a flagship client’s private docs appear in unrelated users’ “Suggested” panel for 20 minutes. The incident is quietly labeled “UX confusion” and buried. - Jonah sends Mira a cryptic Figma prototype titled “Grey Thread” with hotspots linking to anonymized session replays that show AutoDraft scraping email headers from connected accounts to juice recommendations. Jonah disappears from Slack the next morning and his contractor email is deactivated. - Daniel receives an anonymous tip with a CSV of suspicious MAU spikes from “LATAM SMB” accounts. He traces them to a NYC shell vendor, Sable Ridge Partners, that funnels “pilots” to QuillForge. The shell shares a mailbox domain with Marco’s side LLC. - Mira and Daniel cross paths at a DUMBO coffee shop after Evelyn (the bank’s counsel) arranges a “design feedback” meeting. They clock each other’s motives. Attraction undercuts distrust.
