Genre: Contemporary romantic adventure / feel-good fiction 
Place: Paris, France (Montmartre—steep stairs by Rue Foyatier, Place du Tertre before dawn, Square Marcel-Bleustein-Blanchet overlook, Madame Colette’s attic of projectors and reels; Canal Saint-Martin—lock bridges at Écluses des Récollets, sunset on Quai de Jemmapes, barge “L’Abeille” repurposed as a floating screen; Le Marais—Henri & Lila’s risograph shop on Rue Charlot, hidden courtyard strung with mismatched bulbs; Left Bank—Latin Quarter side streets, Shakespeare & Company mezzanine note exchange, riverside bouquinistes where a salvaged speaker is found; Parc des Buttes-Chaumont—Temple de la Sibylle ridge line for dawn sound-capture and a suspended footbridge rehearsal; métro Line 2 and Line 11 buskers; bridges of the Seine—Pont des Arts twilight test; day trips to Giverny—water garden at opening bell, train station café with chipped cups, a clandestine field recording in the poppy meadow; Reims—chalk cellars of a champagne house used for echo tests, Cathédrale Notre-Dame nave resonance mapped at vespers) 
Time: Present day (spring into early summer—cherry blossom confetti in April, a week of capricious gauzy rain, then a humid June heat snap; key nights marked by passing storms and citywide Fête de la Musique) 
Theme: Spontaneity; serendipity; creative courage; the city as a catalyst; love as collaboration; friendship as anchor; tech vs. art synergy; choosing wonder over plans 
Tone: Effervescent, witty, warm, cinematic, hopeful 
Point of View: First-person past with occasional epistolary inserts (voice notes, texts, festival emails) 
Characters: Ishita Gupta, a vibey artsy girl who decided to major in CS for fun as she saw code as a creative medium but didnt realise that the coding and SV totally destroyed her soul and she is learning to be a girl again
