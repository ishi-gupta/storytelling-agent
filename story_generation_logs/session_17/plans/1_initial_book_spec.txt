Genre: Contemporary realism (upmarket/literary)
Place: Paris, primarily Canal Saint-Martin, Belleville, Montmartre, the Latin Quarter; opening in Brooklyn, NY
Time: Present day, early spring (March/April), one week trip
Theme: Impulse vs. planning; expectation vs. reality; urban solitude; the logistics of travel; language and class; screens vs. street-level life
Tone: Wonderlust, Magical, Girly, like someone experiencing something transformative
Point of View: First person past, highly descriptive, and charecter focused
Characters:  - Sally, 27, Brooklyn copywriter stuck ghostwriting influencer captions - Lila Ortiz, 28, Maya’s pragmatic friend/roommate - Craig Donnelly, 40s, Maya’s boss who cares about metrics - Sylvie Martin, 50s, Maya’s Paris Airbnb host, nurse on night shifts - Étienne Dubois, 30, grad student neighbor, intermittent translator with his own deadlines - Samir Benali, 40, café owner near Canal Saint-Martin - Camille Laurent, 29, museum guard/photography student (brief connection, not a fairy tale) 
Premise:  - After a breakup and a bad performance review, Maya doom-scrolls and streams a subtitled, romanticized Paris film. Half-drunk on boxed wine, she books a budget flight and a tiny Airbnb near Canal Saint-Martin for seven nights. - She lands at CDG to a partial transit strike; tries the RER, bails after confusion, overpays for a taxi. The Airbnb is smaller than the photos; the shower floods. Sylvie apologizes but can only drop towels between night shifts.
