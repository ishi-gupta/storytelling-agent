Genre: Contemporary literary fiction
Place: Stanford University campus and the Bay Area, California
Time: 2024–2028 (from arrival through graduation)
Theme: Friendship under pressure; immigrant/international student realities; class and culture gaps; meritocracy myths; mental health; identity and belonging
Tone: Clear-eyed, unsentimental, quietly tender; wry humor; no melodrama
Point of View: Alternating close third-person on Ines and Ishita 
Characters: Ines Almeida—Angolan‑Portuguese, first‑gen international on full aid, Symbolic Systems major, sharp observer masking money stress; Ishita Rao—Indian, middle‑class from Hyderabad, Bioengineering pre‑med, high achiever with quiet anxiety and visa pressure; Mateo Park—Korean American CS roommate, startup‑driven, charmingly selfish; Dr. Leila Farzaneh—acerbic PI, brilliant and transactional; Zadie Kim—RA from Fresno, mental‑health advocate running on fumes; Yifan Liu—international club treasurer, OPT/visa whisperer; Carlos Gutierrez—Dining hall lead, immigrant mentor figure; Naomi Feld—student journalist poking holes in meritocracy myths; Arjun Mehta—VC‑backed dropout recruiting aggressively; Dean Charlotte Klein—well‑meaning but metrics‑obsessed administrator; Teresa Almeida—Ines’s mother in Lisbon, practical and stretched; Domingos Almeida—uncle in San Jose auto shop, offers couch and reality checks; Priyanka and Sanjay Rao—Ishita’s parents in India, loving, status‑conscious, insist on “safe” choices; Omar Haddad—unionizing PhD who complicates lab loyalties; Asha Menon—blunt grad TA who quietly steers both women toward boundaries
Premise: Over four years at Stanford, two women—Ines, a first‑gen scholarship student from Paraguay, and Ishita, an Indian international on a visa clock—build and strain a friendship as they chase labs, internships, and belonging, colliding with class gaps, meritocracy myths, mental health breaks, and an ethics fallout that forces a hard choice about who they’ll be and where they can live after graduation.
