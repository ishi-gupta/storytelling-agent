Genre: Contemporary literary fiction 
Place: Stanford University campus and the Bay Area, California—EVGR and Stern dorms; Huang Engineering Center; Clark Center/Bio-X (Farzaneh Lab); Green Library/Coupa Café; Arrillaga Dining; Cantor Arts/Rodin Garden; Main Quad/MemChu; Meyer Green; Lake Lagunita in drought; Hoover Tower shadow as time marker; Vaden Health Center; CAPS waitlists; Terman Fountain; Searsville/SLAC trails; Redwood City campus shuttles; Caltrain to San Francisco (SoMa AI offices; Dogpatch bioincubators); San Jose auto shop on Monterey Road (Domingos); Daly City and Fremont immigrant enclaves; East Palo Alto family houses with multigenerational rent stress; South Bay mega-church parking lots used for job fairs; startup demo nights in Palo Alto garages. 
Time: 2024–2028 (from arrival through graduation)—2024 election tension on campus; 2025 atmospheric river flooding parts of Santa Clara County and campus basements; recurring late-summer wildfire smoke and N95 lines at CAPS; 2026 AI/biotech hiring spikes with visa caveats; 2027 H‑1B lottery misses and day‑1 CPT temptations; rolling tech layoffs that make “safe” internships precarious; student worker union actions affecting labs; escalating OPT scrutiny at customs in 2028. 
Theme: Friendship under pressure; immigrant/international student realities; class and culture gaps; meritocracy myths; mental health; identity and belonging—tested through concrete constraints: FAFSA-like aid audits; OPT clock math; lab authorship fights; housing insecurity; work-study rules; quiet but chronic anxiety meeting institutional indifference. 
Tone: Clear-eyed, unsentimental, quietly tender; wry humor; no melodrama 
Point of View: Alternating close third-person on Ines and Ishita 
Characters:  - Ines Almeida—Angolan‑Portuguese, first‑gen international on full aid; Symbolic Systems major; brilliant pattern‑finder who takes notes like transcripts; cleans tables at Arrillaga nights to cover remittances; ashamed of borrowing Domingos’s 2008 Corolla; code style elegant but sparse; allergic to performative networking; scar on wrist from kitchen shift accident she hides with a hair tie. - Ishita Rao—Indian, middle‑class from Hyderabad; Bioengineering pre‑med with a wet‑lab crush; lives by color‑coded Google Calendar; visa pressure as constant background hum; prays quietly before exams; keeps a spreadsheet of CPT/OPT scenarios; good at protocols, bad at saying no; panic shows as nosebleeds in dry seasons. - Mateo Park—Korean American CS roommate; optimizes life like code; pitches a privacy‑eroding dorm‑analytics startup; “nice” until resources conflict; runs on Soylent and VC validation; dates Zadie briefly, then poaches her RA data for A/B testing without consent. - Dr. Leila Farzaneh—acerbic PI, brilliant and transactional; runs organoid‑signal processing collab with a stealth startup; offers authorship like currency; believes “impact first, ethics committee later”; relentless Slack pings at 1 a.m. - Zadie Kim—RA from Fresno; mental‑health advocate who prints her own crisis flyers when budget cuts hit; quietly pays for residents’ meal swipes; insomnia masked with jokey memes; the only one who notices both women slipping. - Yifan Liu—international club treasurer; OPT/visa whisperer who runs illegal‑but‑kind office hours on CPT letters; pragmatic about bending rules but draws a line at fraud; knows which employers won’t sponsor. - Carlos Gutierrez—Dining hall lead; immigrant mentor; teaches knife skills and boundaries; calls out Stanford niceness as “HR with better merch”; introduces Ines to union meetings without sermonizing. - Naomi Feld—student journalist; relentless on numbers; follows the money from lab grants to cap tables; writes the piece everyone shares and nobody wants to be in. - Arjun Mehta—VC‑backed dropout; recruits aggressively from dorms; offers Ishita a “research internship” that is really regulatory evasion; charming, never emails—only Signal. - Dean Charlotte Klein—well‑meaning, metrics‑obsessed; talks “belonging scores” while cutting counseling hours; loves dashboards, fears headlines. - Teresa Almeida—Ines’s mother in Lisbon; practical, stretched; voice notes at odd hours asking about rents and saints; sends sardines and guilt in equal measure. - Domingos Almeida—uncle in San Jose auto shop; offers couch, cash advances, and the sentence “a job that needs your visa owns you.” - Priyanka and Sanjay Rao—Ishita’s parents in India; loving, status‑conscious; measure risk in visas and wedding prospects; keep forwarding H‑1B success stories. - Omar Haddad—unionizing PhD; calm in crisis; refuses to fudge data; forces lab to pick sides; costs everyone time and saves someone’s conscience. - Asha Menon—blunt grad TA; teaches “how to say no” scripts; insists boundaries are a skill; gets them through IRB training without shortcuts. 
Premise: Over four years, Ines (Angolan‑Portuguese, first‑gen on full aid) and Ishita (Indian international on a visa clock) become friends in a Frosh 101 section after a dorm fire alarm strands them at Meyer Green at 2 a.m.; their bond tightens over shared exhaustion and diverges under different constraints. Year 1: Ines juggles dining shifts and a campus IT helpdesk job while Mateo’s startup poaches dorm data; Ishita lands a spot in Farzaneh Lab doing organoid imaging, learns to pipette on trembling hands, and starts logging her hours against visa rules; wildfire smoke cancels classes, Zadie runs ad‑hoc therapy circles when CAPS waitlists spill. Year 2: A flashy AI/biotech collab between Farzaneh and Arjun’s stealth company pressures Ishita to “preprocess” data that improves a Nature‑bound figure; Omar flags missing controls; Ines builds an ethics‑audit tool for Naomi’s investigative piece on lab‑to‑startup pipelines, catching a grant conflict that implicates Farzaneh; Carlos leads a quiet dining walkout that feeds the campus with donated burritos. Year 3: A storm floods the Clark Center basement freezers; Ishita salvages samples and becomes indispensable; Arjun offers her a “CPT‑compatible” role that skirts labor rules; Ines, broke after a financial‑aid “verification review,” couch‑surfs at Domingos’s shop office, showers at Arrillaga, and writes code for Mateo under‑the‑table until she realizes he’s reselling students’ mental‑health app data to test ad targeting; Naomi publishes, triggering an inquiry; Dean Klein pivots to reputation containment; Zadie unravels after a resident crisis and finally asks for help. Year 4: The lab’s paper is accepted with Ishita mid‑author list; Omar files a formal complaint attaching Ishita’s Slack metadata; Asha coaches Ishita through a precise, documented refusal to sign off on a supplementary methods tweak; Farzaneh threatens to pull recommendation letters; Arjun dangles an offer with “day‑1 CPT” and a shadow H‑1B plan; Yifan warns it’s a trap that will kill future consular renewals. Ines’s audit tool becomes the backbone of the university’s “integrity dashboard,” credited to a dean’s task force without her name; Naomi leaks emails showing the dashboard repackaged her prototype; Ines must choose to go public and risk aid repercussions or take a quiet industry role in SoMa; Ishita must choose between reporting the lab’s doctored preprocessing (and risking visa fallout and blacklisting) or staying silent to keep the OPT‑to‑H‑1B pipeline alive. The friendship fractures over their choices, then reforms—not around agreement, but around the hard, adult act of witnessing each other’s costs. Graduation happens in smoke‑thick air under Hoover Tower, with one staying on STEM OPT, one leaving the Valley for a job that doesn’t require a badge swipe to belong.
