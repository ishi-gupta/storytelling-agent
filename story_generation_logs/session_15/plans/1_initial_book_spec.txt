Genre: Literary microfiction 
Place: A cramped Bronx apartment kitchen; a nearby community garden plot 
Time: Present day, one growing season (late winter to early fall) 
Theme: Attention to small things; labor and care; food insecurity; community; cycles of growth and use 
Tone: Spare, unsentimental, quietly tender 
Point of View: Close third person focused on Mara 
Characters: Mara Ortiz (28, line cook, exhausted but meticulous); Luis Ramos (10, curious upstairs neighbor); Mrs. Chinyere Okoye (62, retired nurse, garden coordinator) 
Premise: Told in micro-chapters of exactly five words each. Mara pockets a lone pinto bean from a torn bag at work. She soaks it in a jar, then starts it on her windowsill. Luis notices and asks questions; Mrs. Okoye offers soil and a plot. They transplant the seedling; a late frost threatens it; they improvise protection with a cut bottle. A teen tags the garden fence; Mrs. Okoye organizes cleanup; Mara keeps watering. The bean flowers; a heat wave hits; the plant wilts; Mara hauls water after shifts. A single pod matures; they harvest and save two seeds. Mara cooks the remaining beans simply with onion and oil; she shares a small bowl with Luis and Mrs. Okoye on the stoop. Winter returns; the seeds wait in an envelope labeled “Next time.”
