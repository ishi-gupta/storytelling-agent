Genre: Contemporary fantasy, culinary drama with procedural environmental stakes and slow-burn psychological thriller beats 
Place: Copenhagen, Denmark—restaurant in a converted shipfitter’s hall on Refshaleøen facing the windward harbor cranes; foraging across Øresund shoals off Dragør, eelgrass beds near Saltholm (permit-only), illegal peeks toward restricted Peberholm from a RIB at dawn; back-of-house alley in Christianshavn for staff smokes; Kalvebod Fælled’s brackish ditches in late summer; a rented marine lab bench at DTU Aqua, Lyngby; winter service runs past the silent Paper Island construction; Leif’s apartment above Torvehallerne with air purifiers; a municipal hearing room at Teknik- og Miljøforvaltningen; harbor baths at Islands Brygge after a sewage overflow alert; spring finale on a slick skerry under the Øresund Bridge during a perigean spring tide 
Time: Present day, one year—opens March equinox king tide; May pop-up; August heatwave and harbor algae advisory; October reviewer awards cycle; December staff exodus season; January herring run; February investor summit; closes on the following March supermoon spring tide 
Theme: Ambition vs. ethics in a scene that monetizes rapture; authenticity vs. spectacle in tasting-menu theater; exploitation of a habitat framed as “Nordic purity”; addiction recast as memory-seeking; taste as involuntary archive vs. consent; scarcity engineered as brand 
Tone: Grounded, sensory, tense, unsentimental; kitchen realism, cold-water fieldcraft, bureaucratic friction 
Point of View: Close third person limited (Alma’s perspective), with brief interludes of second-person taste notes that trigger precise, intrusive autobiographical scenes 
Characters: Alma Sørensen—32, head chef; ex-line cook at Relæ until a blackout on service; two years sober, coins taped in knife roll; sleepwalks during menu changes; obsessive with saline gradients; wants a star but not a dining room tour; collects diners’ forgotten napkins; Signe Hald—45, coastal biologist/forager; keeps tide charts on paper, won’t harvest roe; divorced, lives on a houseboat with a weather log; refuses to disclose coordinates; holds a scientific permit and uses it as a boundary; Leif Ravn—50, reclusive star critic with lingering anosmia post-viral; carries a pocket nebulizer; hears taste words like notes; his smell returns only during Alma’s skær course and only while eating; writes in fragments; Kaspar Lindholm—38, venture-backed restaurateur; leases the hall, NDA culture, talks “rollout to Dubai” and “IP around memory”; installs an unlicensed recirculating tank in the basement; sees scarcity as a launch narrative; Mateo Ruiz—29, sous-chef; ex-Kitchen Confidential reader who stayed kind; keeps HACCP logs religiously; builds a salinity probe from parts; refuses to plate skær for pregnant guests; Freja Kjær—34, sommelier; sheet of terroir maps in her locker; starts microdosing skær to “tune pairings,” drifts into dependence; Einar Poulsen—22, dishwasher-turned-commis from Nuuk; logs tides on his phone; notices torn eelgrass and dead pipefish; pushes for catch limits; Ingrid Sørensen—62, Alma’s mother with early-onset dementia; remembers sea smells but not faces; brief lucidity with salted foods; Petra Østergård—41, rival chef/influencer; weaponizes “sustainability” in captions; poaches staff with stock options; tries to replicate skær with agar and psilocybin tincture 
Premise: - During a predawn forage with Signe on a skerry at negative tide, Alma tastes a translucent filamentous algae—“skær”—that blooms only under a rare spring tide and lunar phase. Dishes seasoned with microscopic shavings trigger vivid, involuntary autobiographical memories in diners—specific, personal, often ecstatic. - A 14-course pop-up serves “salted kohlrabi, skær, dill pollen”; patrons weep, call ex-lovers, tip like zealots. Leif Ravn returns twice in one week; his anosmia recedes while eating Alma’s menu, and he publishes a career-making, cryptic rave. - Kaspar demands secrecy, NDAs, and scale; he installs a clandestine skær tank fed with harbor water. The pump backflush during a storm spreads fragments into the canal; days later, harbor fish die-offs and a municipal swim advisory hit the news. - Freja begins microdosing skær pre-service “to hear acidity,” nails pairings, then doubles doses; a diner relives a car crash on the pairing course and bolts; a complaint triggers a quiet Fødevarestyrelsen inspection. - Signe, seeing torn eelgrass and trampled skerries near Saltholm from copycat foragers, refuses to supply; Alma fabricates skær shavings from kombu and salt to pause harvest without telling Kaspar; the decoy fails—diners feel nothing—and a VIP threatens a lawsuit over “withheld experience.” - Petra poaches Mateo and two servers, launches a lookalike dish with faux-skær; a blogger exposes her psilocybin hack; the scandal splashes the whole scene, and Kaspar spins it as proof Alma’s is “the only authentic memory cuisine.” - Einar documents habitat damage and the illegal tank; he hands footage to Signe and a sympathetic DTU postdoc; a city hearing opens on permit violations. Kaspar offers Einar a raise and a title to keep quiet. - Alma serves skær to Ingrid at home, chasing one unbroken memory; they get ten lucid minutes about Alma’s father on the Dragør pier. The crash afterward—Ingrid’s agitation—breaks Alma’s abstinence for a night; she returns to work raw and determined to end it. - Leif’s second review, a spare, numbered list, hints at culpability and calls the menu “a theft from a place that cannot consent.” Sales surge anyway; Kaspar books Dubai scouts. - Final spring tide: Alma, Signe, Mateo, and Einar boat out under the Øresund Bridge. Petra’s team lurks on another RIB. Alma harvests one last tuft, then, on service that night, plates a course called “Return”—eelgrass broth, cold smoke, no skær. She reads a one-page note about methods and limits, publishes coordinates and the moratorium, and invites regulators in. Kaspar pulls funding mid-service; half the room walks; the other half stays. - Coda: Leif eats the no-skær menu; his smell does not return. He writes the last review anyway. In the interludes, second-person taste notes shift from ecstatic recall to clear, grounded present-tense: salt, steel, wind.
