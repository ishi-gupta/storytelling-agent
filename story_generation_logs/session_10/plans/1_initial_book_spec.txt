Genre: Contemporary fantasy, culinary drama
Place: Copenhagen, Denmark—harbor, foraging islands, and a 24-seat tasting restaurant
Time: Present day, one year spanning spring to spring
Theme: Ambition vs. ethics, authenticity vs. spectacle, exploitation of nature, addiction, memory and taste
Tone: Grounded, sensory, tense, unsentimental
Point of View: Close third person limited (Alma’s perspective), with brief interludes of second-person taste notes
Characters: Alma Sørensen—32, head chef, ferocious palate, recovering alcoholic; Signe Hald—45, coastal biologist/forager, Alma’s field partner, wary of overharvest; Leif Ravn—50, reclusive star critic with lingering anosmia; Kaspar Lindholm—38, venture-backed restaurateur pushing secrecy and scale; Mateo Ruiz—29, sous-chef, loyal, ethical brake; Freja Kjær—34, sommelier, starts microdosing skær; Einar Poulsen—22, dishwasher-turned-commis from Nuuk, tracks tides, flags damage; Ingrid Sørensen—62, Alma’s mother with early-onset dementia; Petra Østergård—41, rival chef/influencer poaching staff
Premise:  - During a predawn forage with Signe on a skerry at negative tide, Alma tastes a translucent filamentous algae—“skær”—that blooms only under a rare spring tide and lunar phase. Dishes seasoned with microscopic shavings trigger vivid, involuntary autobiographical memories in diners—specific, personal, often ecstatic. - A 14-course pop-up serves “salted kohlrabi, skær, dill pollen”; patrons weep, call ex-lovers, tip like zealots. Leif Ravn returns twice in one week; his anosmia recedes while eating Alma’s menu, and he publishes a career-making, cryptic rave.
