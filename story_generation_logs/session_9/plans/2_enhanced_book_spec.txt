Genre: Contemporary romance with corporate mystery 
Place: New York City (Flatiron, DUMBO, Hudson Yards, LIC)
Time: Present day (2024–2025)
Theme: Trust vs. ambition; privacy and data ethics; the intimacy of work; love under scrutiny - How promotions and valuations distort consent. - The seduction of “anonymized” data and the lie of frictionless productivity. - Romance built in late‑night standups, tested by compliance timelines. - Choosing who to protect when disclosure burns equity and relationships. 
Tone: Grounded, tense, witty; tech-realistic - Real tools (Jira, Slack, Looker, Snowflake, Okta), accurate legal/process friction (DPAs, DPIAs, BAAs), plausible failure modes (S3 misconfig, service account drift, token scope creep). - Dry humor in sprint rituals; tension in badge logs, calendar crumbs, and log redactions.

Characters: Maya Chen—31, Spindle director of product for Sprintboard; wants VP before IPO; quietly approved a shadow data capture to juice demos; Aaron Wolfe—34, due‑diligence lead at Apex Ridge (Series C); ex‑privacy engineer; embedded to vet risk, drawn to Maya, suspects off‑book data; Victor Hale—Spindle CEO, valuation‑obsessed; Leila Farooq—CTO, ships fast, trims ethics; Dani Park—Head of Data Science, summarizer ingests “anonymized” calendars; Rishi Patel—Security lead, blocked from product logs; Ava Morales—GC, pushing consent overhaul, sidelined; Jonah Kim—rival PM angling for Maya’s role, leaks internally; Sloane Mercer—tech reporter circling a misuse story; Felix Domínguez—Apex partner pressuring Aaron to bless the deal; Elena Volkova—CEO of data vendor ClearLambda with murky sourcing; Tom Reyes—IT admin at a marquee client flagging meeting‑scrape anomalies; Priya Desai—Maya’s roommate, ER nurse, blunt outside‑tech check.
