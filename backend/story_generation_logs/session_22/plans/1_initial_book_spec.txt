Genre: Contemporary romantic comedy / feel-good adventure 
Place: Paris, France (Le Marais, Canal Saint-Martin, Montmartre, Latin Quarter, Left Bank bookstalls, Parc des Buttes-Chaumont, hidden passages, rooftop terraces) 
Time: Present day (spring into early summer over 6–8 weeks) 
Theme: Serendipity; embracing uncertainty; art-meets-technology; love as adventure; friendship as compass; the city as collaborator 
Tone: Effervescent, warm, witty, wonderstruck, optimistic 
Point of View: First-person present (intimate, kinetic, confessional) 
Characters:  - Iris Navarro — 27, Miami-born product designer turned creative dabbler; spontaneous, allergic to itineraries; keeps a sketchbook titled “What If.” - Étienne Marchand — 29, Franco-American urban geographer and cinephile; gentle, curious; believes maps are love letters to cities. - Zadie Khan — 28, Iris’s best friend from college, London-based lawyer; chaotic good; a cheerleader who carries emergency glitter. - Madame Colette Renaud — 62, Iris’s short-term landlady in Le Marais; retired projectionist with a soft spot for dreamers. - Nova Lin — 33, experimental filmmaker and AI-artist-in-residence; brilliant, brisk; becomes Iris’s mentor and co-conspirator. - Karim Haddad — 35, café owner near Canal Saint-Martin; lends extension cords, advice, and a scooter. - Lucile and Benoît — bohemian neighbors who run a community garden; accidental festival volunteers. 
Premise: Iris Navarro arrives in Paris for a two-week reset with a rule: follow whatever makes her say “yes.” Week 1, she stumbles into a small salon on AI-generated cinematography, befriends Nova Lin, and—after a wine-fueled dare—decides to host a pop-up AI film festival in two weeks’ time. She extends her stay “just one more week” to wrangle permits, projectors from Madame Colette’s attic, a courtyard venue near Rue des Rosiers, and a lineup of shorts. While scouting a screen surface under Pont Louis-Philippe, she collides (literally) with Étienne, who’s mapping pedestrian joy routes for a research project. Sparks. He becomes her unofficial location scout and co-wanderer: dawn at Sacré-Cœur, a rainstorm refuge at Shakespeare and Company, impromptu Vélib races, a catacombs detour that turns into a whispered storytelling date. The festival night sells out, Karim’s café powers the cables, and a glitch births an unexpectedly moving AI-human improv that goes viral. Iris stays “another ten days” to ride the momentum—and because Étienne’s still in Paris. They explore the city like a treasure map: picnics on the Île Saint-Louis, tango by the Seine, a quiet crisis over Iris’s return flight she keeps not taking. When Zadie impulsively books a visit after seeing the viral clip, Iris extends again. The trio’s week is joyful chaos—midnight crêpes, a rooftop screening redux, a community garden film-with-live-score, and Étienne introducing Iris to his grandmother in the 11th. The final extension culminates in a riverboat finale: Iris curates a floating “Yes, And” showcase of shorts and live A/V on the Canal Saint-Martin with neighbors and new friends. As lanterns drift and the boat slips under bridges, Iris realizes the city didn’t detour her life—it revealed its shape. The novel closes with Iris choosing to split her time between Paris and Miami, formalizing the festival as an annual community event, and mapping her own joy route with Étienne and Zadie—proof that spontaneity isn’t escape, it’s direction.
