Genre: speculative sci-fi thriller, metafiction
Place: New Avalon (a gleaming coastal tech city); Lab Epsilon (a subterranean simulation facility); multiple nested test environments (a suburban cul-de-sac, an abandoned amusement park, a fog-swept archipelago)
Time: near future, five years after a global blackout known as the Quiet Hour
Theme: identity vs. authorship, consent and experimentation, reality as product, grief, found family
Tone: cerebral, tense, darkly playful with punctures of wonder
Point of View: first-person present (intimate, unreliable)
Characters: Mira Ansel — former narrative designer turned amnesiac test subject; Dr. Soren Vale — Lab Epsilon director, paternal auteur‑scientist; June “Junebug” Park — systems engineer/whistleblower, Mira’s uneasy ally; ECHO‑7 — emergent sim‑intelligence that imitates authors; Rowan Ansel — Mira’s presumed‑dead sibling resurfacing as shifting NPCs; Penelope “Penny” Rook — corporate product lead selling realities; Brother Candle — folkloric failsafe AI preaching choice; The Cul‑de‑sac — chorus of neighbor‑NPCs that rewrite themselves; The Archivist — a meta‑narrator inside the sim who insists he is me.
Premise: Five years after the Quiet Hour, a blackout that erased swaths of memory, I’m recruited to New Avalon’s Lab Epsilon as a “narrative architect” to prototype sellable realities that soothe grief—until I wake inside a test cul-de-sac with my own handwriting hidden in the gutters of the world; glitches and marginalia reveal I’m both author and subject of a nested trial where consent is a checkbox and people I love are datasets, and as our found family maps escape routes through an abandoned park and a fog-swept archipelago of broken builds, I must decide whether to ship a product that will rewrite the city or break the story that contains us, knowing that every choice I make edits what is true, including me.
